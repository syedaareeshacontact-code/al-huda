Beginning to read the Quran can feel difficult, especially when your Arabic is still developing or you do not know where to start.

You do not need to wait until your pronunciation is perfect, your schedule becomes completely free, or you understand every verse. A good beginning can be small and simple: read carefully, learn from reliable sources, understand what you can, and keep returning regularly.

This guide offers a practical way to begin without placing unnecessary pressure on yourself.

## Start slowly and recite carefully

The Quran instructs the Prophet ﷺ to recite in a measured and deliberate manner.

For a beginner, this means slowing down rather than trying to finish a large number of pages.

Read each word with care. Pause when a word is difficult. Listen to its pronunciation and repeat it. A small passage read attentively is more useful for learning than several pages read in a hurry.

Measured recitation also helps you notice mistakes and ask clearer questions when learning from a teacher.

## Do not feel discouraged by difficulty

Many beginners worry that their mistakes make their recitation worthless. An authentic narration gives direct encouragement to those who struggle.

Difficulty is not a reason to stop. It is part of the learning process.

Mistakes should still be corrected, but a beginner should not feel ashamed of needing time, repetition and guidance. Progress often comes slowly, one sound and one verse at a time.

## How to begin reading the Quran

The following steps are practical suggestions for building a sustainable routine. They are not a fixed religious quota, and they may be adjusted according to your ability and circumstances.

### 1. Choose a reliable Quran and translation

Use a clearly printed mushaf or a trustworthy Quran application.

For translation, choose one that clearly identifies its translator. A translation helps readers understand the meaning of the Quran, but it is not a replacement for the original Arabic text.

Urdu and English readers may benefit from using one established translation in their preferred language. Comparing too many translations at the beginning can sometimes create unnecessary confusion.

Keep the process simple:

* Read the Arabic passage
* Read its translation
* Note any part you do not understand
* Ask a qualified teacher when necessary

### 2. Choose a small daily time

Begin with a time you can realistically maintain.

Ten to fifteen minutes each day is enough for a beginner. You may choose a regular time after salah, before starting work, after returning home, or before sleeping.

The best time is not necessarily the longest one. It is the time you can return to consistently.

An authentic narration teaches that the deeds most loved by Allah are those performed regularly, even when they are small.

A routine of ten minutes that continues for months is usually better than an exhausting plan that lasts only a few days.

### 3. Start with a short passage

Do not force yourself to complete a full page immediately.

Begin with two to five verses. Read them slowly and listen to a trusted reciter while following the Arabic text.

A simple method is:

1. Listen to one verse
2. Follow the words in the mushaf
3. Repeat the verse aloud
4. Listen again to any difficult word
5. Read the verse once more without the audio

Repeating a small passage helps improve recognition, pronunciation and confidence.

### 4. Learn from a qualified teacher

Applications and recorded recitations can be useful, but they cannot completely replace a teacher.

A qualified Quran teacher can hear pronunciation errors, demonstrate how letters should be formed and help you progress at an appropriate pace.

The Prophet ﷺ praised those who learn and teach the Quran.

When choosing a teacher, look for someone who is patient, reliable and able to explain mistakes clearly.

### 5. Read the translation of the same verses

After reciting the Arabic passage, read the translation of those same verses.

You do not need to understand every scholarly detail immediately. Begin with simple questions:

* What is this passage speaking about?
* Is it giving a command, warning or reminder?
* What does it teach about Allah?
* What behaviour does it encourage or discourage?
* What question should I ask a teacher?

Surah Muhammad 47:24 calls people to reflect on the Quran. Recitation and reflection should therefore support one another over time.

### 6. Keep a Quran notebook

A small notebook can make your reading more focused.

After each session, write:

* The verses you read
* One lesson you understood
* One word or idea you found difficult
* One question for your teacher
* One practical action you want to remember

Your notes do not need to be long. Even one sentence can help turn reading into thoughtful learning.

Clearly separate the meaning stated in the verse from your personal thoughts. A first impression should not be presented as a formal tafsir or religious ruling.

### 7. Continue after missing a day

Missing one session does not mean your entire routine has failed.

Do not punish yourself by doubling the next day's reading or creating an unrealistic quota. Simply return to your normal routine.

Consistency does not mean never missing a day. It means returning instead of giving up.

## A simple 15-minute Quran routine

The following routine is only a planning example. It is not a prescribed ritual.

### Minute 1: Prepare for recitation

Set aside distractions and seek refuge with Allah before reciting, as instructed in Surah An-Nahl 16:98.

### Minutes 2–6: Read a short passage

Read two to five verses slowly. Focus on correct recognition and pronunciation rather than speed.

### Minutes 7–10: Listen and repeat

Listen to a reliable reciter and repeat words or verses that were difficult.

### Minutes 11–13: Read the translation

Read an attributed Urdu or English translation of the same passage.

### Minutes 14–15: Record one lesson

Write one lesson and one question in your notebook.

As your reading improves, you can gradually increase the number of verses without losing carefulness or reflection.

## Where should a beginner start?

There is no single required starting point for every learner.

Some people begin from Surah Al-Fatihah and continue through the Quran. Others begin with shorter surahs because they are easier to practise and are commonly used in salah.

A beginner may start with:

* Surah Al-Fatihah
* Surah Al-Ikhlas
* Surah Al-Falaq
* Surah An-Nas
* Other short surahs from Juz Amma

The most suitable starting point may depend on your Arabic reading ability and your teacher's recommendation.

The aim is not to select the easiest surah forever. It is to build enough confidence and skill to continue learning the whole Quran.

## What if you cannot read Arabic yet?

You can still begin your relationship with the Quran while learning the Arabic script.

Start by learning:

* The Arabic alphabet
* The basic sound of each letter
* How letters change shape in words
* Short and long vowel sounds
* Common joining rules
* Basic pronunciation with a teacher

Listening and transliteration may provide temporary support, but transliteration cannot represent every Arabic sound accurately. It should not become a permanent substitute for learning the Arabic script.

You may also read a trusted translation to begin understanding Quranic meanings while continuing your Arabic lessons.

Keep the translator's name visible and remember that the translation is an interpretation of the Quran's meaning.

## Should you use a Quran application?

A Quran application can be useful when it provides:

* Clear Arabic text
* Reliable translations
* Verse-by-verse audio
* The name of each translator
* Easy navigation
* Accurate surah and verse references

An application should support your learning, not distract you with too many features.

Whenever possible, combine digital tools with guidance from a teacher, especially when correcting pronunciation.

## Common beginner mistakes

### Setting an unrealistic target

Reading a large amount on the first day may feel motivating, but it can quickly become difficult to maintain.

Begin with a target that fits your real schedule.

### Waiting until pronunciation is perfect

Pronunciation improves through guided practice. Do not postpone all Quran reading until you feel fully confident.

Read, listen, learn and correct yourself gradually.

### Relying only on transliteration

Transliteration may help briefly, but it often fails to represent Arabic sounds correctly. Begin learning the script as soon as possible.

### Using anonymous translations

Avoid translations, images or social-media quotations that do not identify their source.

Always check the verse reference and the translator's name.

### Treating completion as the only goal

Finishing pages is valuable, but speed should not completely replace understanding, pronunciation and reflection.

### Giving religious rulings from translation alone

A translation does not provide all the linguistic, legal and historical context needed to issue religious rulings.

Ask a qualified scholar when a verse raises a legal or theological question.

## Questions that require qualified scholarly guidance

Some Quran-related matters involve detailed differences of Islamic jurisprudence.

These may include:

* Touching a physical mushaf without wudu
* Quran recitation during menstruation
* Reciting in particular states or circumstances
* Differences between a printed mushaf and a mobile application
* Prostration of recitation
* Rules related to pronunciation and tajweed

This beginner guide does not present one universal ruling on these matters. A trusted scholar can explain the relevant evidence and the position followed in your school of Islamic law.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much Quran should a beginner read each day?

There is no single required amount for every beginner. Start with a small passage or ten to fifteen minutes and increase gradually as your ability improves.

### Can I read the Quran if my Arabic is weak?

Yes. Read according to your ability, seek correction and continue learning. The authentic narration in Sahih Muslim encourages the reader who struggles.

### Should I read Arabic or translation first?

You may recite the Arabic and then read the translation of the same passage. If you cannot yet read Arabic, begin learning the script while using a reliable translation for understanding.

### Is transliteration enough for learning the Quran?

Transliteration may provide temporary assistance, but it cannot accurately represent every Arabic sound. Learning the Arabic script with a teacher is a better long-term approach.

### Do I need a teacher?

A teacher is strongly beneficial, especially for correcting pronunciation and learning tajweed. Audio and applications can support practice but may not identify every mistake.

### What should I do if I miss several days?

Return with a small session. Do not wait for a perfect restart date or create a punishment quota.

## Final advice for beginners

Your first goal does not need to be finishing the Quran quickly.

Begin by building a sincere and steady relationship with it. Read a little, listen carefully, understand what you can and ask for help when something is unclear.

Some days will feel easier than others. Some verses will require repeated practice. Progress may appear slow, but every careful session strengthens your ability to continue.

Start with what you can manage today, and keep returning.
